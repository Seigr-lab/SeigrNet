EXPERIMENT / DECLARED 24 SEPTEMBER 2026

State transitions and acquisition timing.

Direct and omitted-trigger transitions, subsequent readings and held-state references.

CONTEXTElectrical statesCalibrationSEIGRASM acquisitionTrigger-control evidence

Question

Do triggered observations follow a changed control while untriggered reads retain the preceding result, and how do transition history, added delay and repeated acquisition affect six-state recognition? The preceding return met its trigger-status criterion but retained one wrong and ten unresolved triggered classifications. This experiment separates direct transitions from transitions preceded by a diagnostic read.

Declared method

Three separately acquired boots on the same apparatus. Each boot contains six blocks and 3,240 observations: 1,296 training observations and 1,944 held-out diagnostic observations. All values below are decimal unless explicitly prefixed. Retain control codes 90, 93, 96, 99, 102 and 105, the two-count calibration guard, operating limits, bus deadlines and restoration path from the preceding experiment.

Each block begins with 36 consecutive triggered training readings at each state, using a 1,000 microsecond delay and alternating ascending/descending state order by block. Freeze its calibration before any validation. A rejected calibration retains all raw acquisition with recognition unavailable; its outcome cannot count as successful recognition.

Each block then tests all 36 ordered pairs A → B, including the six unchanged-state pairs. Every pair has nine observations in two sequences. Both sequences begin with their own triggered A reference. The direct sequence therefore measures the first B trigger without an intervening diagnostic read.

For zero-based block b and pair position p, enumerate pair (5p + 7b) mod 36, with A its quotient by six and B its remainder. Select delay index (p + b) mod 3. If p is even, execute stages 0–3 followed by 4–8; otherwise reverse the two sequences. Every pair sees each of the six delay/order combinations once per boot, and each block contains 18 instances of each sequence order. This is a deterministic balanced schedule, not randomized sampling; the full ordering remains available for assessment.

Record actual control-selection, ADC-call and trigger-slot counters. The named delay is the wait before ADC setup, not a claim of exact selection-to-conversion latency. Stage 6 includes the preceding stage-5 diagnostic transaction and cannot be treated as a direct 1 ms transition. A 20 ms hold is a comparison condition; its physical sufficiency is not assumed.

All modes retain ADC reset/configuration, the observed status sequence and two result-pair reads. Stages 5 and 8 omit the trigger and cannot enter native recognition. The remaining 1,512 held-out triggered observations are recognized against that block's frozen bands. The 432 omitted-trigger observations retain their raw values and explicitly unavailable recognition. Hardware, operating-condition or transport failure preserves the attempted prefix and ends acquisition with the existing restoration attempt. Restore the original control after every completed block.

Predefined assessment

Require complete recovery of all 3,240 observations and all six block restorations, successful acquisition and operating checks, matching requested/initial/final control values, complete diagnostics and agreement between both result pairs. Triggered observations must retain status octets 0 → 1 → 0; omitted-trigger observations must retain 0 → 0 → 0. Missing or failed observations cannot satisfy a criterion.

Retention and update: in every one of the 216 paired trials, stage 5 must equal the preceding A reference at stage 4, and stage 8 must equal the preceding B result at stage 7. For the 180 trials with A different from B, stage 6 must differ from stage 4. The 36 unchanged-state trials impose no numerical-change requirement on a newly triggered conversion. Retention and numerical update are assessed separately from the correctness of the recognized state.

Six-state recognition: all six calibrations must be valid, and all 1,512 triggered held-out readings must be correctly recognized. Count wrong, unresolved and unavailable readings separately for every stage and selected delay. The per-boot combined criterion requires both retention/update and recognition criteria. A three-boot recognition claim requires the complete predefined sequence to qualify on all three boots; one successful boot establishes only its observed result.

Secondary comparisons examine first versus subsequent B readings, held-state references versus first-transition readings, early versus later blocks, sequence order and requested predecessor. Preserve all training extrema, raw octets, timing and operating observations. No warm-up observations are discarded, no band is widened after seeing validation, and no failed first reading is replaced with a later reading. Timing effects and drift remain hypotheses to test against these observations.

The outcomes test whether the measured results respond to the controlled trigger/change sequence. They do not independently establish analog sampling time, absolute ADC accuracy or exclusive device ownership. A separately validated observation path remains necessary for independent analog confirmation.

Three-boot assessment

The retention/update relation met its predefined criterion on all three boots. Across the series, all 648 A-retention checks, all 648 B-retention checks and all 540 changed-request numerical-update checks passed. Independent analog conversion freshness, absolute ADC accuracy and exclusive device ownership remain unproven.

The three-boot recognition criterion was not met. All boots contained unresolved readings, and boot 2 additionally rejected its first calibration. The series contains no wrong assigned identities where recognition was available, but this cannot qualify recognition while 24 triggered readings remained unresolved and 252 remained unavailable.

Of 540 first direct readings in changed-state pairs, 510 were available and correctly recognized; 30 were unavailable in boot 2. Considering all first direct B readings, including unchanged-state pairs, stage 1 produced 609 correct, 3 unresolved and 36 unavailable results. Stage 6, the first trigger after omission, produced the same aggregate counts, with all three unresolved outcomes in boot 3. These observations do not establish a superior sequence, causal timing effect or independent trials.

Failures in unchanged-state and held-state observations, together with the overlapping training intervals in boot 2, remain unexplained by a simple shortage of delay immediately after a requested state change. Boot 3's four later blocks passed, but later failures remained in boots 1 and 2. A general warm-up remedy is therefore unqualified. No bands were widened and no failed or unavailable observations were discarded.

Next investigation: characterize reported raw-code variation during repeated acquisition at a held request, retaining training extrema and complete acquisition diagnostics. Compare any revised acquisition or recognition rule on new held-out data. Physical variation, calibration coverage and acquisition-path effects must remain distinct hypotheses; independent analog sampling evidence is still required. The completed series does not select a revised recognition rule.

The reproducible series assessment retains per-boot outcomes, separate triggered and omitted-trigger counts, stage comparisons and the original qualification limits.

Execution path and dependencies

Existing ROM/EEPROM entry loads project-authored literal AArch64 SEIGRASM instructions. The existing launch, GPIO/I2C access, bounded PMIC acquisition, supervision, calibration, restoration and SGDATA01 SD recording remain operative. The new instructions enumerate matched transitions and select diagnostic mode and delay. Recognition excludes explicitly untriggered observations. PC tooling copies literal opcode words, places data and validates saved evidence; it does not implement target execution or invoke a foreign target compiler or assembler. Existing boot-firmware dependence remains unresolved.

All six control values, the permitted control range and hardware failure guards remain unchanged. ADC status transitions and response to control changes are operational evidence; they do not independently establish analog sampling time or absolute ADC accuracy.

The transition operation supplies sequence and sample-dispatch entries. Acquisition entry 0x3b0 extends the shared operation at two dispatch words and appends a third entry; earlier entries retain their counts and recognition behavior. The new entry retains 64-word rows and excludes diagnostic mode 2 from recognition. The PC return reader independently reconstructs scheduling, status, retention and recognition assessments from the saved journal. Configuration schema 5 retains the declared geometry and permutation. Diagnostic schema 2 is unchanged. In held-out rows, fields 5, 6 and 7 contain delay index, ordered pair identity and logical stage; training retains its existing storage-group meanings.

The maximum journal occupies 20,784 of 32,704 reserved sectors, leaving 11,920 empty reservations. Target record writing remains SEIGRASM-owned and precedes the boot partition. Earlier evidence and its frozen sources remain unchanged. Execution validation checks the full schedule, balanced coverage, mode/delay dispatch, negative-control recognition exclusion, failure restoration and connected entry-to-journal behavior under supplied devices.
